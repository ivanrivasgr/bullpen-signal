# ADR 0020 - Resolution via dbt Double Emission, Superseding the Replay Re-Emission of ADR 0019

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-06-11
- **Supersedes:** the mechanism of ADR 0019 (not its intent)

## Context

ADR 0019 decided how the system records whether a projected batter was right once the lineup confirmed. Its intent — close the revision loop so the should-have-fired ledger captures both the held-back decision and its later validation or reversal — was correct and is unchanged. Its mechanism was wrong, and implementation exposed two flaws that this ADR corrects.

ADR 0019 said the replay emits the resolution: when the uncertainty window closes, the replay would emit a second matchup signal per uncertain pitch, carrying the real batter, and the revision producer would compare the pair. That description assumed the replay generates matchup signals. It does not.

## What implementation revealed

**Signals are generated in dbt, not in the replay.** The replay emits PitchEvents to Kafka. Those flow through Flink to bronze, then through silver_pitch_events and silver_matchup_events, and the matchup signal itself is generated by silver_matchup_signals, a dbt Python model that applies signals.matchup_signal to each row. The replay never constructs a MatchupSignal. So "the replay emits a second signal" was not implementable as written — the replay has no signal to emit. A resolution expressed as a second PitchEvent would also have collided on the bronze natural key, forcing a discriminator column through the entire bronze and silver chain.

**The first approach destroyed ground truth.** ADR 0019's mechanism section noted that apply_uncertainty_window "replaces it with the projected batter for the emitted event". That replacement overwrote batter_id with the projection, discarding the observed batter. Once the real batter was gone from the event, no downstream model could compare projection against truth — the very comparison the revision loop depends on. This was the deeper flaw: the resolution was impossible to compute downstream because the data needed to compute it had been destroyed upstream.

## Decision

The resolution is produced by dbt, not by the replay, and the observed batter is never destroyed.

Two changes, landed across commits 189baae, 111d517, and 37fb353, implement this.

First, preserve observation. batter_id is always the observed batter from Statcast. A new nullable field projected_batter_id carries the inference the system would have made during the uncertainty window. It is null for confirmed pitches and null when projection fails. This field is threaded end to end: PitchEvent, the Avro schema, the bronze Iceberg schema, the Flink job, and the silver models. Inference never overwrites observation.

Second, emit twice in dbt. silver_matchup_events joins handedness for the projected batter as well as the real one, producing projected_handedness_matchup alongside handedness_matchup. silver_matchup_signals then emits two signals for each uncertain pitch: a reduced-confidence signal computed from the projected handedness (what the system knew during the window) and a full-confidence signal computed from the real handedness (the resolution once the lineup confirmed). A confirmed pitch, or an uncertain pitch with no projection, emits one signal. The revision producer — unchanged, exactly as ADR 0019 predicted — sees the pair per uncertain pitch and applies detect_revision.

The revision classification is now keyed on handedness, not batter identity. If the projected and real batters share a handedness, the signal_value is identical and the pair is a no-op (the projection held in every way the signal can see). If their handedness differs, the signal_value differs and the producer emits a material_update. This is a subtlety ADR 0019 did not anticipate: it framed the comparison as projected-batter versus real-batter, but the signal only depends on handedness, so two different batters with the same handedness produce no revision.

## Why dbt, reversing ADR 0019's rejection of "alternative 3"

ADR 0019 rejected a dbt model for the resolution on two grounds. Both fall away once observation is preserved.

It argued a dbt model "reconstructs in batch what the streaming path does in flight" and would be thrown away when the Flink streaming job lands on 2026-06-20. But signal generation already lives in dbt for the batch path; the resolution belongs wherever signal generation lives. The streaming job will reimplement signal generation in Flink regardless — the dbt double emission is no more throwaway than silver_matchup_signals itself, which the streaming job will also supersede. Putting the resolution anywhere other than dbt would have split signal-generation logic across two components.

It argued a dbt model "produces revision rows that never passed through Kafka". This conflated two things. The dbt double emission produces signal rows, not revision rows. The revision producer still reads those signals and publishes revisions to Kafka exactly as before. Every revision in the lakehouse still passed through the topic. The property ADR 0019 wanted to protect is intact.

ADR 0019's true objection — that the replay already held the real batter, so a separate process would re-derive it — was correct about a separate confirmation process (its alternative 2) but did not apply to dbt. dbt is not re-deriving anything: it reads projected_batter_id and the real batter_id, both now preserved on the event, and computes both signals from data already present.

## Verification

End-to-end on 2024-04-15 with the lineup cache spanning 2024-04-13 through 2024-04-15 (so projection has a previous-game lineup to draw from), uncertainty forced to every game:

- 59 uncertain pitches, each with projected_batter_id populated.
- silver_matchup_signals materialized 459 rows: 400 full-confidence, 59 reduced-confidence, with 59 pitches carrying two emissions each.
- The revision producer emitted 43 revisions and recorded 16 no-ops. The 43 are pitches where projected and real handedness differed (material_update); the 16 are where they agreed (baseline_confirmed, identical signal_value). 43 + 16 = 59, one per uncertain pitch.
- All 43 revisions confirmed present in the features.matchup.v1.revisions Kafka topic.

## What ADR 0019 still governs

The streaming Flink job scheduled for 2026-06-20 remains its own future ADR. The batch path is now dbt; the streaming path will generate signals and resolutions in Flink. The intent both ADRs share — record what the system would have corrected once truth arrived — carries forward unchanged.

## Consequences

- The natural key for silver_matchup_signals includes lineup_state_at_emission, since a pitch legitimately produces two rows. ADR 0019 anticipated this consequence correctly even though its mechanism was wrong.
- The reconciliation summary's correction_rate is now interpretable as designed: the ratio of material_update to baseline_confirmed by handedness matchup is the calibration signal Phase 3 feeds back into the ADR 0016 placeholder magnitudes.
- The replay engine gained no resolution logic. The resolution-emission unit tests ADR 0019 promised for the replay were never written, because that code was never built; the contract is instead locked by the silver_matchup_signals model and its natural-key test, and by the preservation tests on apply_uncertainty_window.
